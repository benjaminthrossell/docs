Your role

Your job

You're the warmth between the programming, and the early warning.

I write the training and the bot watches the clock. You're the person who makes a Forge+ member feel someone is actually paying attention. You also catch the ones going quiet, and make sure nothing slips through without me knowing.

What you're responsible for

  • Be around in the group channels: react to what people post and join a chat or two.
  • Give a public well-done to one or two people each session.
  • Look after the people on your list that day.
  • Follow up anyone falling behind on their workouts, or on their end-of-season check-in. That's a big part of why you're here.
  • Tell me early if something feels off.
  • In week 1 of each season, follow up members who haven't started the block.
  • At the end of each season, chase end-of-season check-ins and lead the messages that follow.

Who you look after

Your list covers Forge+ members and Fat To Jacked clients.

Forge+ membersEverything in this handbook.
Fat To Jacked clientsThey can appear on your list too. Be part of their group chat and do the follow-ups on your cards. Their weekly check-ins are mine, so you don't comment on those.

What you're not responsible for

You're not their coach. You don't give training, diet or results advice, you don't fix the app, and you don't decide who stays or goes. You'll never be judged on any of that. What's mine lists what to pass on.

  • You don't need to go looking for work. The list comes to you in the brief.
  • You don't keep records by hand. The brief reads the inboxes and the training numbers for you.
  • You don't write as me. You're you. Be warm and consistent, without cheerleading.

What good looks like

  • Members reply to you, and little conversations start.
  • The people drifting get noticed before they disappear.
  • I hear about problems early, from you.
  • Your list stays short. If it keeps wanting more than five people, something upstream is wrong, so tell me.

Your week

You're in on Tuesdays and Fridays.

  • Each session, your brief lands in #client-success by 10am.
  • Follow-ups go to members in their client inbox channels.
  • You don't need me to check your messages before they go.

A normal session

  1. Read the brief, top to bottom. It tells you what today looks like before you open anything else.
  2. Drop into the group channels. React to what people posted, and add to a chat or two. That means #forge and #coaching.
  3. Give your well-dones. One or two people, in public.
  4. Work through your list. Finish each opener in your own words and send it to that member's client inbox channel.
  5. Flag anything that's mine. Do it now, not at the end of the week.

If you're unwell or away

  1. Tell me as soon as you can, in #jenny-herrero.
  2. Post a short note for members in the group channels, saying you're unavailable, sorry if anyone is waiting, and when you'll be back.

Your list carries over. Anyone waiting on you moves to the top of your next brief.

If the brief doesn't arrive

Tell me in #client-success. Until it arrives, do your group channels and reply to anyone you know is waiting.

Times

Times in your brief are Perth time.

Still to settle with me

Your exact hours. Ask me at handover, and this page will be updated.

If someone replies between sessions

Pick it up on your next session. Anyone waiting on you goes to the top of your next brief, so they won't get lost. This includes anyone held from your last brief.

Your season

A season runs for six weeks, and the next one starts the day after.

Week 1Follow up members who haven't started the block. Progress photos are due on the Wednesday.
Weeks 2 to 5Your normal Tuesdays and Fridays.
End of the seasonChase end-of-season check-ins, then lead the messages that follow.

Progress photos

Progress photos come up once a season.

  • Chase on day 2 and day 4 after they're due, then stop. If either day isn't one of your working days, use your next session.
  • If someone owes photos and a check-in, make it one ask.
  • If they've sent some, thank them first, then ask for what's missing.
  • Ask when, not if.

End of season

End-of-season check-ins are the one point where members should hear from both of you. You lead, and I follow.

  1. Chase anyone who hasn't filled theirs in.
  2. When a member's check-in comes in, they appear on your list as a card that includes what they wrote.
  3. Message them in your own words.
  4. I see the same card and follow up as their coach.

If a check-in raises something that's mine, tag me rather than answering it.

You don't post members' check-ins anywhere. If more come in than fit on one brief, the extras move to your next session.

Your role

How it's measured

Three things tell us the role is working.

  1. Are more people sending in their check-ins and photos?
  2. Are people actually engaging with you?
  3. Is it saving me time?

What else we keep track of

We also keep a record of how long people wait to hear back from you, so we can spot it if people are being left waiting. It allows for you working Tuesdays and Fridays.

It looks at how long a member waits for your first reply, not how many messages you send or how you split them up. One long reply counts the same as several short ones.

What we don't track

Your online status isn't tracked. Nobody is watching whether you're green in Discord.

The work

The brief

It's a short stack of cards. Read them in order.

You only get cards for the people who need to hear from you that day, so everything in the brief is relevant.

  1. Welcome card: your standing job, a reminder that the rest of today's agenda is in the cards, and a heads-up if the information isn't live.
  2. A nice one: someone worth a public well-done.
  3. Who needs you: 3 to 5 people. Each card says what's going on, what we're after, their current numbers, and a half-finished opener.
  4. Wrap-up: who's held for next time, who's off your plate, and your next session.

What a card looks like

An example. Sam is made up.

Sam: open ask (3 days)

Works night shifts and mostly trains after work. Said last week that sleep has been the hard part.

What we're after: Get a reply going, then find out when the photos will be in.

Live: training 9/13 last 30 days

hey Sam, [your words]… (open with something real, then the ask)

Build on the starter in your own words. Never paste it.

Reading it line by line

  • The title line says why they're on your list today.
  • The first lines are worth knowing. They help you sound like you remember the person, because you do.
  • What we're after is the point of your message.
  • Live is their current numbers.
  • The opener is where you start writing.

The opener

The opener is a starter, not a script. Finish it in your own words and never paste it as is. Cards marked "lighter-touch" are gentle hellos, not chases.

"Handle with care"

If you see this on a card, go gently. Ask how they're doing first and let them lead.

"Held for next time"

The list stops at five so your session stays short. Anyone held moves to your next brief. You don't need to do anything about them today.

If a card looks wrong

Tell me. If a card isn't clear enough, that's on the brief, not on you, and I'll sharpen it.

Who lands on your list

Someone lands on your list if they're waiting on you, they've skipped a week of training, they've gone quiet, or an ask has sat too long.

The list is already filtered, so you only see people who need a word. If a card looks wrong, tell me. That's a fault in the brief, not in you.

What puts someone there

They're waiting on youThey messaged last and you owe a reply. These always come first.
Skipped training7 days with no workout logged. Send a warm, private check-in.
An open askYou asked something and it's been over 48 hours. One gentle nudge.
Once a seasonPhotos or an end-of-season check-in that haven't come in, or a review that has.

Quiet days

You'll always see at least three people. If fewer than three really need you, the rest are lighter-touch hellos. There's no ask behind them, and no rush.

Busy days

You'll never see more than five. The rest are held for your next session, and the wrap-up card names them.

Who won't be on it

  • Anyone I'm working on re-engaging myself. The wrap-up card names them.
  • Anyone I've asked you to hold off on. I speak first.
  • Anyone quiet by nature, unless they're waiting on you.
  • Anyone properly gone quiet: 14 days with no training and no reply. They go to me.

The work

Writing to members

Keep it chill and natural, like pinging someone you know and want to help.

One warm line beats a paragraph. You don't need to mention the past in every message. Just let it show that you remember and you care. Write as yourself, not as me.

The shape of a message

  1. Greeting and praise, or something real about them.
  2. A line break.
  3. The ask, on its own line.

Two or three short chunks at most. If you've written two compliments, cut one. If they've told you something in chat, like their work, family or a trip, work it in.

Trimming a draft

  • Cut anything forced. If a line reads like a pitch, take it out.
  • Use one softener at most. "No stress" or "just a friendly nudge", not both.
  • Cut lists of detail. Say the thing that matters, not everything you know.
  • Go light on emojis.
  • Use callbacks. A member's own words said back to them land best.

An example

Sam is made up.

Too much

Hey Sam! Just a quick one to check in. You've been doing amazing, and I'm so proud of how consistent you've been, you're honestly such a pleasure to have in the group and I love how much effort you put in, so I wanted to see if you might be able to get your progress photos in sometime soon if that's okay?

Better

Hey Sam! Night shifts and 9 sessions is seriously solid.

When can you get your progress photos in?

Ask when, not if

"Could you do this?" gets a maybe. "What day suits?" gets a day.

Two-step messages

Sometimes the right move is to check in first and ask once they reply. The card will tell you. If someone has already replied to you, a follow-up ask can go straight in the same thread. It counts as their one open ask, so there's no 48-hour wait.

Do you need to send drafts to me?

No. Send your messages yourself. That's part of how this role saves me time.

The rules

Keep these every time.

1

open ask per person as the normal rule. A second only when needed. Never a third.

before you expect anything back.

2 days

at least, between nudges to the same person.

14 days

no training and no response: it goes to me. Stop chasing.

1

compliment at most. Short chunks, no walls of text.

Public

praise. Nudges stay private.

Keep messages relaxed and natural. One warm line beats a paragraph. Some members are quiet by nature: notice them, don't nudge them.

Why these exist

They're there so nobody feels buried. A member with three things open from you stops opening your messages.

One open ask

Most of the time, one thing at a time is enough. There will be moments when something else really does need chasing, so a second ask can happen, but be slow to do it. There's never a third.

Holding off

If someone already owes you a reply, or replied in the last 48 hours, hold off on anything new. If you're not sure, wait for your next session.

The one exception

Holding off is about starting something new. If the member has replied and you're answering them, carrying on that conversation is fine, and a follow-up ask can go in the same thread with no 48-hour wait. It counts as their one open ask.

Where things go

When you're stuck

Check here first. If it isn't covered, ask me in #client-success or by DM. Asking always beats guessing.

A DM to me and a message in #jenny-herrero are both fine for anything private.

What to do, in order

  1. Search this handbook. The search box is at the top left.
  2. Ask the bot, if your question is about a specific member.
  3. Ask me in #client-success or by DM.
  4. Make sure it's logged. Tell me the handbook didn't cover it. I'll add the answer here, at the same link.

How to ask

  • Give your options and your pick. "I see three ways to do this, and I'd go with the second" is the best kind of question.
  • Group your questions. Send them together where you can.
  • Tell me if you need a back-and-forth. A call is also an option.

How fast you'll hear back

For questions through the day, allow about an hour. If you need me there with you for a while, tell me when.

Emergencies

If a member seems to be in real danger, or something serious happens, come straight to me. Message me in #jenny-herrero and tag me, and don't wait for your next session.

When something's missing here

If this handbook didn't have your answer, that's a gap in the handbook, not a gap in you.

Notes about the handbook

When something here is unclear, missing, or doesn't work in practice, log it with the note box. Select any text and press Add note, or use the Add note button in the corner of each page.

  1. Log it as you go. Notes are saved on your device, under My notes in the menu.
  2. On Friday, send them all at once. Press Copy for Friday and paste the summary into #client-success, with how you'd fix each one.
  3. Anything urgent goes straight to me. Don't save it for Friday.

Notes stay on the device and browser you saved them in, so use the same one each time.

Handle with care

Injuries

You can check on people's injuries. Knowing how someone's going is a big part of looking after them.

  • If you think it needs me, or someone's having trouble training because of it, tell me.
  • Don't promise that I'll look at it or fix it. I'll usually point them to a physio.
  • Don't give advice on it yourself.

What that looks like

Sam is made up.

Sam says their knee has been sore

You: Oh no, how's it been this week? Is it stopping you training?

Then: If it's getting in the way of training, tell me in #client-success.

Check in before you ask

If someone's carrying something, ask how they are first. Make any other ask only once they've replied.

Don't use the usual line

"Let me get Benjamin on that" isn't for injuries, because it sounds like a promise that I'll fix it. Tell me in #client-success instead.

Avoid saying

  • "Benjamin will sort this out for you."
  • "Benjamin will look at your program."
  • "Try resting it for a week."

Handle with care

Going quiet

Catch them early, try a second route, then hand them over.

7 daysNo workout logged. A warm, private check-in.
14 daysNo response for 14 days, through both routes. Hand them to me, and stop.

The whole sequence

  1. Your ask. One message in their client inbox channel, with one ask.
  2. One nudge, once 48 hours have passed with no answer.
  3. Leave it a week.
  4. Try a second route. If they're still training, message them in Everfit. If they're not training, tell me in #client-success instead.
  5. Log it. Note each contact in #client-success, so there's a record of every try.
  6. At 14 days with no response through either route, hand them to me in #client-success, and stop.

If they've also stopped training, the 7-day check-in is your nudge.

Why training is yours to raise

If someone's falling behind on their workouts, say so, kindly. That's what you're there for. What you don't do is tell them how to train. That part is mine.

After the hand-off

The wrap-up card shows who's with me, so you don't chase them by accident. Those people are off your plate.

What's mine

Tag me in #client-success, not in the member's channel.

Never raise these

Don't bring up warnings, downgrades or removals with a member, even if you've heard them talked about. If a member raises money or leaving, tag me and leave it with me.

If something feels off

Don't sit on it. Flag it to me in the moment.
